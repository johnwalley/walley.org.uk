Last month I put together a quiz game: [Inspector Oars](https://www.inspectoroars.co.uk/). The intended audience is pretty niche; people involved in rowing at Cambridge and Oxford universities. You’re given the name of a college and asked to pick the rowing oar design used by that college from a selection.

<Image src={inspectorOars} alt="Inspector Oars quiz question asking which of four oar blade designs belongs to Christ’s College. The score is 3, on question 4 of 20." />

An obvious question you might ask is which are the easiest and hardest designs to identify? Well, we have over 1,200 games played and the results are in…

<Image src={chart1} alt="Bar chart of the five most recognised oar designs, each bar ending in the college’s blade: St Catherine’s College 95%, Girton College 94%, Caius 92%, Clare Hall 92% and Queens’ College 92%." style={{ backgroundColor: 'white' }} />

Topping the charts is St Catherine’s College. This distinctive design features a Catherine Wheel and is one of the few which doesn’t require any rowing (or college) knowledge to make an educated guess at.

Second through fifth are all similar in design, with vertical coloured lines against a uniform background.

Now to the five designs which players found toughest.

<Image src={chart2} alt="Bar chart of the five least recognised oar designs: Wolfson College 80%, Trinity Hall 80%, First and Third Trinity 79%, Homerton College 78% and Murray Edwards College 77%. Three of the blades are a single plain colour." style={{ backgroundColor: 'white' }} />

Murray Edwards College comes bottom of the table. Followed by Homerton College, First and Third (Trinity), Trinity Hall, and Wolfson College. It’s no coincidence that three of the five designs are a single colour, and fairly non-distinctive colours too. I also wonder if Trinity Hall and First and Third being so similar led to some confusion and wrong answers.
